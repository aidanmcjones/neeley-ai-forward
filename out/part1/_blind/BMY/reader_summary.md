# BMY blind read: reader summary

## The company in five lines

1. Bristol Myers Squibb: 2025 revenue $48,194M, flat on 2024 ($48,300M); Eliquis $14,443M is the largest product (BMY:10k_FY2025.md p48; Q4 2025 release p6).
2. 2026 guidance was raised in July to $49.0-50.0B revenue and $6.75-7.00 non-GAAP EPS, mostly because Eliquis growth went from 10-15% to 20-25% (2026-07-30 release p1, p6).
3. That Eliquis jump is a pricing event, not mainly a volume one. After a U.S. list price cut and the December 2025 MFN deal (Eliquis free to Medicaid, tariff relief until January 2029), the U.S. gross-to-net rate fell from 52% to 34% in Q2 2026 (10-Q 2026Q2 p31).
4. Income below the operating line is shrinking. The AstraZeneca diabetes royalty ($1,121M) ended on 2025-12-31, and the Keytruda and Tecentriq royalties ($334M in H1 2026) end on 2026-12-31.
5. In the FY2025 10-K, policy risk moved from "could" to "has": the IRA, 340B, the MFN agreement and possible renegotiation of the maximum fair price are now written as realized drags.

## Three strongest candidates

1. **BMY-c03, Footnotes (high confidence): the royalty cliff.** About $1.76B of 2025 pretax royalty income ends within 12 months. The 2027 piece is Keytruda plus Tecentriq: about $668M a year (H1 2026 of $334M times two), or about $0.27 EPS, roughly 4% of the 2026 EPS midpoint. Merck's own 10-Q confirms the Keytruda royalty ends after 2026. None of the four latest calls mentions the end date.
2. **BMY-c04 and BMY-c07, Gross-to-net and Peer contrast (medium confidence): the Eliquis price reset.**
   - In Q2 2026, Medicaid and Medicare rebates fell $2,757M (down 61%) and gross sales fell 18%, but net product sales rose 6%.
   - About $1.44B of the 2026 Eliquis guidance raise comes from this rebate-formula effect.
   - J&J did not cut Xarelto. Using pharmacy acquisition cost (NADAC) as the list-price proxy, Eliquis now sits at 1.36x its Medicare negotiated price and Xarelto at 2.83x, which makes Eliquis about 44% cheaper outside Medicare.
   - 2025 revenue already included $485M of prior-period rebate reserve releases.
   - The deal terms are confidential.
3. **BMY-c02, Earnings-call Q&A (medium confidence): the call and the filing disagree on Part D.**
   - On the Q3 2025 call (grade B), Lenkowsky said the Part D redesign was "roughly equal" positives and negatives on a net basis.
   - The 10-Q filed the same day (U.S. gross-to-net 48% to 53%, nine months) and the FY2025 10-K (49% to 53%) name Part D redesign and government channel mix as the primary driver of the higher U.S. rate. That is about $2.9B of extra 2025 U.S. deductions.
   - J&J's 10-K credits Part D redesign for Xarelto's growth.

Other candidates:

- **BMY-c01, 340B language drift (medium).** The 10-K wording on 340B changed from "could have an adverse effect" to "has negatively affected". In the same year, charge-backs grew 22% against 5% gross sales growth: up to about $1.9B of extra deductions, an upper bound.
- **BMY-c06, Abecma no longer broken out (medium, small in dollars).** Abecma ($427M in 2025) was folded into "Other Growth products" from the Q1 2026 10-Q.
- **BMY-c08, Eliquis label (medium).** Eliquis has fewer indications than Xarelto but can be used in dialysis and severe renal impairment, where Xarelto's label says to avoid use. This fits Eliquis's roughly 3x lead in Part D enrollees.
- **BMY-c05, receivables (low).** Gross trade receivables rose 14% while revenue was flat. The jump is mostly a 91% rise in the charge-back reserve, not channel stuffing.
