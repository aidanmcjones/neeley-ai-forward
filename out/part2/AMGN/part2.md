## Evidence

Decider: regulator (FDA, CDER, in the TAVNEOS withdrawal hearing).

### Why it lands

The decider is the FDA's drug center in a withdrawal proceeding. Amgen's own 10-Q lists postmarketing commitments or studies among the actions the FDA may take, framed as an alternative to withdrawal, and the FDA's stated concern is the reliability of the ADVOCATE data, where 9[8] of the 331[8] pivotal patients were re-adjudicated. Amgen's submissions so far are retrospective (real-world evidence and a blinded re-adjudication); a prospective, independently adjudicated trial answers the reliability concern going forward, and a U.S. liver registry speaks to the hepatotoxicity warning the label already carries[2][21][22][23].

### What the evidence can tell

The evidence can tell that the FDA's option set includes postmarketing commitments, that the hearing request is filed, that TAVNEOS stays on the market while the process runs, and that if a hearing is not granted the FDA may enter summary judgment[2][1][9][14].

### What it cannot tell

It cannot tell whether the FDA would accept commitments in lieu of withdrawal: Amgen says it cannot predict whether the FDA will find its submissions sufficient; the only indexed precedent (LUMAKRAS) is an FDA-imposed confirmatory study after a Complete Response Letter in an accelerated-approval case, not a bargain; and no trial can answer the proposal's second ground, that the application contained untrue statements of material facts. Nothing indexed sizes the probability, the hearing date or how the CHMP recommendation weighs[21][24][25][26].
