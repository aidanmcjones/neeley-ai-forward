# PFE strategist summary (Part 2, Decide)

## Position in five lines (from out/part1/PFE/part1.json)

1. Finding 1: the U.S. ATTR-CM class grew $1,246M (+47%) in H1 2026 while U.S. Vyndaqel was flat at $1,975M vs $1,976M; its class revenue share fell from 74.8% to 50.8%, partly from Pfizer's own payer-contract price cuts.
2. Finding 4: product revenue deductions rose from 38.6% to 41.3% of gross sales in 2025 (the guided IRA Part D redesign step), flat at about 40.0% in H1 2026.
3. Finding 5: Ibrance's Medicare price ($7,871 vs $15,741 list, 50% lower) and its U.S. basic patent expiry both land in 2027; the CEO called the NPV hit very small.
4. Finding 2: an $842M Q2 2026 legal charge (vs $422M), with the Depo-Provera MDL deal called not material; state-court cases sit outside it.
5. Finding 3: contingent consideration rose to $2,259M after a $550M Metsera-driven fair-value increase (non-cash, obesity milestone odds up).

New fact surfaced while costing (not in Part 1): Vyndamax Part D formulary listings rose from 84 of 331 (2025Q4 file) to 192 of 328 (2026Q3 file), but combined tafamidis Part D claims in Q1 2026 (44,532) were 4.0% below the 2025 quarterly average (46,381). Generic Vyndamax is settled for June 1, 2031, later than the end-2028 loss the CEO assumed on the 2025Q4 call.

## My ranking

1. PFE-m01: the best-evidenced economics (per-patient value from CMS and the 10-K, label-backed promo with a full claims map) and it attacks finding 1 directly; weakness is that management already says diagnosis is its focus (partly announced), so the synthesizer must sell the size and the 2031 tail, not the idea.
2. PFE-m02: the most non-obvious move, resting on a pattern in CMS data (listings up, claims not) that management's own "contract to keep 90% access" line does not address; low confidence because product gross-to-net is undisclosed and only one 2026 claims quarter exists.
3. PFE-m03: strategically the right question (what replaces tafamidis after June 2031), but the return is two assumptions deep, the comp is a different modality, and Intellia's filings are not indexed.
4. PFE-m04: easy and cheap, but close to what management already signals and sized on an assumed promotional share.

Rejected before writing: a tafamidis new indication (no trial can read out inside 24 months; U.S. hATTR-PN would need a new study); a litigation move on Depo-Provera state cases (finding 2; no cited dollar exposure to cost against); a Vyndamax list-price cut modelled on Bristol Myers Squibb's Eliquis cut (product gross-to-net and the Part D discount base are not in the index).
