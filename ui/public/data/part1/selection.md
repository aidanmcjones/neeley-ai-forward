# Part 1 company selection (blind ranking)

Ranked on the case's own test: findings that are material (big enough to change an investor's view) and not obvious (not the starter thread, a patent cliff, a press-release headline or something management said plainly), plus one product from the listed competitive set that compares well against its rivals and ties to a financial finding, plus at least five honest, cited, sized rows. Inputs were only the case PDF, the rubric, and the ten blind synthesis.json and reader_summary.md files. No league table, needle file, red-team note or other worktree was read before this file was sealed.

## Picks

### 1. AMGN (Amgen)

Amgen is the only company with two survivors that are high on materiality, low on obviousness and high on confidence at the same time, and no other name has even one survivor graded that way at high confidence. The best item, AMGN-c06, is a $2.4B TAVNEOS intangible ($2.3B U.S.) at about 5.2x FY2025 TAVNEOS sales of $459M, carried against an FDA withdrawal proposal, with the recoverability sentence weakening between the Q1 and Q2 2026 10-Qs and no call, slide or release stating the carrying value; a full U.S. write-off is about $4.2 a share pre-tax. Behind it, AMGN-c02 shows sales deductions rising from 52.5% to 54.4% of gross sales and an accrual build that gave 2025 operating cash flow about $1.07B more support than 2024's, attributed in part to "timing of payments", with no deduction-rate figure on any searched call. AMGN-c03 (TEPEZZA Q1 2026 growth where 22 of 29 points came from inventory, about a quarter of the company's Q1 product growth, with roughly $62M still in the channel at June 30) is a third, medium-grade survivor. The five-row table fills honestly: three survivors plus cited and sized killed rows such as the IRS Puerto Rico exposure (at least $7.6B net before interest, material but already discussed) and the EVENITY and TEZSPIRE profit share ($1.31B in 2025). The product ruling holds after narrowing: Repatha ($3,016M in 2025, +36%) beats Praluent, Leqvio and Lipfendra on label text the company does not lead with (coronary revascularization in the MACE indication, HoFH from age 10, Lipfendra's 30-minute post-dose fast), and ties to the 10-K's mid-single-digit 2026 net-price guide. It beats the other nine because it is the only name where the needle, the backup rows and the product all clear the test without leaning on an inference.

### 2. MRK (Merck)

Merck has the most narrowed rows of any company (four: MRK-c01, c03, c04, c10), two of which are material and non-obvious, and the best data behind them: its four latest calls are grade A, so "never said on a call" was actually tested rather than assumed. Its best item, MRK-c03, is the least obvious item on any slate: IRS notices seeking about $1.3B of extra transition tax plus about $260M of penalties, a cash exposure of about $1.56B plus interest (about 2.4% of 2025 sales) and a gross GAAP-EPS ceiling of about $0.63 a share, with zero mentions in any call, release, deck or press release and the Q2 2026 10-Q newly doubting an administrative resolution. MRK-c01 adds a long-dated inventory pool that rose from $3.8B to $5.5B to $5.9B and kept growing after the December 2025 deal delayed the tariffs that were its stated rationale. The table reaches five with c04 (Keytruda SC barred in Germany, a dated legal catalyst) and c10 (a 2.5% to 4.0% Medicare drug-line premium for Qlex over IV) plus a cited, sized killed row such as the $3.3B fall in U.S. chargeback and rebate provisions. It ranks below Amgen because both material survivors are medium on materiality and the $0.63 is a ceiling whose overlap with the $2.53B reserve is undisclosed. It ranks above the rest despite a weak product: Keytruda is the starter-thread product and the narrowed edge (Opdivo Qvantig cannot be used with ipilimumab, Qlex has no such carve-out) is real but unsized, and the Imfinzi and Tecentriq side lacks the rival makers' own statements and the Tecentriq SC label.

### 3. PFE (Pfizer)

Pfizer takes third because it is the one remaining company that meets both legs of the test, even though its needle is weaker than Novo's. PFE-c01 is high on materiality and high on confidence: across three filers' 10-Qs, the U.S. ATTR-CM class grew about $1.25B (+47%) in H1 2026 while U.S. Vyndaqel was flat ($1,975M vs $1,976M), so Vyndaqel's revenue share fell from about 75% to about 51%, a figure no Pfizer, Alnylam or BridgeBio call, release or deck quantifies. Its obviousness is medium because the direction of share loss was said on Pfizer's calls; it is a quantification of a known story, not a discovery. PFE-c04 is a second material, non-obvious survivor (a "certain legal matters" charge that doubled to $842M in Q2 2026 with no explanation on the call, beside two different "not material" wordings for Chantix and Depo-Provera), though GAAP-only and with an inferred link. The tie-breaker that lifts Pfizer over Novo is the product: Vyndaqel family against Attruby and Amvuttra is a listed set, it is material ($6,380M in 2025, about 10% of revenue), and it is the only product on the slate that ties directly to the company's best financial finding with three primary revenue lines and three labels lined up on matching quarter ends, which is the comparison work the case says cannot be found in one place. The table fills to five with cited, sized killed rows (the Metsera contingent consideration at $2.26B after a $550M fair-value increase, the flat H1 2026 deduction rate near 40.0% of gross, the Ibrance 2027 Medicare price overlap), and Ibrance itself is avoided as the starter thread.

## Eliminated

- NVO: the closest miss; NVO-c01 (DKK 91.0bn of contract-manufacturer commitments, 71% of 2025 operating profit, about DKK 9.1bn per 10% found surplus) is a strong needle at medium confidence, but it is the only material survivor, its link to the DKK ~3bn charge is inferred, and the only unvoiced product edge favors the rival Foundayo, so the product leg fails.
- AZN: two medium-grade survivors (U.S. chargebacks from 8.4% to 10.8% of gross, about $1.14bn versus the 2023 rate; a recurring "one-off" tax-credit pattern) and only three narrowed rows, while the Tagrisso edge loses the OS comparison (HR 0.75 for the rival) and the IV-infusion argument, with one quotable call.
- BMY: one material, arguably non-obvious survivor (Keytruda and Tecentriq royalties of about $0.62-0.67B a year ending December 31, 2026, about $0.25-0.27 of EPS) that a strict judge could read as an exclusivity loss, and an Eliquis edge that is list-level and unsized.
- ABBV: one medium survivor (a new 2044 Skyrizi-combination royalty tail worth about $1.0-1.5B of present value, bounded not disclosed) and every large theme on the record.
- GILD: one material survivor, a contingent and shrinking $568M pre-commercial Trodelvy inventory (about $0.36-0.37 a share if written down), with the Biktarvy edge unsized.
- JNJ: one conditional survivor (an offshore drug-plant drift worth $0.29-0.54 EPS a year only if the confidential tariff exemption ends in 2029), a product edge that is management's own talking point, and four honest rows short of five.
- LLY: the most-covered name; its one material survivor is a post-approval inventory-days build (about 410 to 469 days, about $2.1B) with no EPS effect, and the other two survivors are a ceiling and an unsized label gap.

## Method

1. Read the case PDF and rubric for the test, then each company's synthesis.json rulings and reader_summary.md data gaps; no other ranking source was opened.
2. For each company, listed survivors (survived or narrowed), their materiality, obviousness and confidence grades, the best candidate and its size, and the product verdict.
3. Weighted the best item first on materiality and confidence together, then on obviousness, and did not rank on the synthesizers' survivor counts alone.
4. Checked the product leg: a listed rival set, an edge that survives the skeptics, and a tie to a financial finding; checked the five-row table using survivors plus killed rows that are cited and sized.
5. Broke close calls (MRK vs PFE vs NVO) on the product comparison and on honest row count, and recorded each data limit that weakens a "never said" claim.

## Limits

- Transcript grades cap the obviousness test for most names: only one quotable call for ABBV, AZN, BMY, GILD, LLY and NVO, so "never said" there is unrefuted rather than confirmed; MRK (four grade-A calls) and JNJ (official PDFs) are the exceptions, and AMGN has only one grade-A recent call.
- Several best items are bounded, not disclosed: MRK-c03's overlap with the reserve, NVO-c01's surplus share, ABBV-c05's tail size, PFE-c04's Depo-Provera share.
- The ABBV and JNJ readers found no Q3 2026 10-Q in the index, and the Tecentriq SC and Rybrevant Faspro labels are absent, which thins the MRK and AZN product comparisons.
- The AMGN reader's note that TAVNEOS sales sit in no filing table was wrong (the 10-K itemizes them); the synthesis corrected it, but other reader data-gap notes were not independently re-checked.
- The MRK-c10 ruling text contains an unrendered template placeholder in its reasoning; the claim's own figures (2.5% to 4.0%) were used.
- This ranking judges the evidence the blind pipeline produced, not the full universe of findings each company may hold; a stronger reader on NVO or AZN could change positions three to five.
