## Winners scope one user, one path, one number, then sell it like a case

Across rubrics, scores compress into the middle. A judge who scored a year of events through July 2026 found that winners separated on things the rubric does not name. A **live working demo was "the single largest score differentiator,"** narrow-and-done beat platform-and-broken, and a team that named its own weakness gained credibility ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). The same source found that framework choice, team credentials, slide decks and polish beyond legibility did not move scores. The analytical frame that "the weakest variable limits the whole score" applies directly to six equal criteria. A broken demo sinks Completeness, and with it the Design and Presentation scores that depend on what judges saw ([DEV: AI hackathon winners](https://dev.to/jsb-securedme/what-ai-hackathon-winners-actually-have-in-common-1kla)).

The scope rule is to name a specific persona, not an industry, and build only the "golden path," the exact screens the demo will show, fixing bugs on that path alone ([lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)). The one-liner template is: **"[Persona] uses [product] to [action], cutting [metric] from X to Y."** Every feature request gets tested against it. A one-day Claude hackathon at WorkOS (39 teams) reached the same lesson: scope to one workflow, because "many features seem urgent but aren't" ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/claude-day-internal-hackathon)).

Winning AI projects in 2025 showed multi-step behavior wired to real data. Microsoft's AI Agents Hackathon was won by RiskWise, a supply-chain risk system that orchestrated several tools and data sources ([Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/AI_Agents_Hackathon/winners/)). London Business School's one-day AI Agent Cup for MBA teams rewarded agents that did real work: AI voice calls to enrich alumni data, homework analysis that generated practice material, and lecture content turned into KPI-driven simulations ([London Business School](https://www.london.edu/news/lbs-ai-agent-cup-2025-highlights-lbs-growing-strength-in-applied-ai)). The test for avoiding a wrapper is blunt: "If you can build it without AI, it's not an AI project," so show a before and after ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/?p=136)). For a business-school case, three shapes fit best. The first is an analyst over the case data that produces a decision artifact (ranked recommendations, flagged risks, a draft memo) with citations back to source rows. The second is automation of a named manual workflow that shows hours saved. The third is a decision dashboard where the AI explains drivers and runs what-if scenarios. Each comes with a natural dollar outcome.

Case-competition norms supply the second half. Losing teams offer the client a menu, while winners commit to one recommendation and say why the alternatives fail ([Roadtooffer](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/consulting-case-competition-guide)). Cornell's Corning rubric gives **30% of the content score to implementation**: timeline, short- and long-term considerations, risks and mitigations ([Cornell](https://business.cornell.edu/centers/ccem/conference/corning-case-competition/)). Executives reject any AI pitch that cannot answer "how much will this make us, or how much will this save us, and by when?" Inflated claims backfire, and bounded ranges read as credible ([Voltage Control](https://voltagecontrol.com/articles/how-to-get-executive-buy-in-for-ai-initiatives-without-overselling/)). The most likely way a technical team loses here is a strong demo with no quantified, committed business recommendation. The most likely way a business team loses is a slide concept with no working AI. Aidan and Parker can cover both halves.

The tooling lessons are just as concrete. A losing entrant in an Opus 4.7 hackathon generated a "production-ready" scaffold on game day, which brought documentation bloat and context poisoning. He packed many tasks into each prompt and submitted "with only seconds to spare" without knowing whether the code worked ([Human Inference](https://humaninference.substack.com/p/why-i-didnt-win-the-opus-47-hackathon)). Torq's second-place team gave each parallel Claude session its own folder, kept hard constraints in an AGENTS.md, and scored outputs with scripts. Two sessions editing the same file erased the speedup ([Shirizly](https://inbarshirizly.substack.com/p/coming-soon)). Keep secrets out of client code (one vibe-coded app's exposed keys led to about $2,500 in card fraud), keep a known-good tagged version, and do not mix code generators on one project ([fuzen.io](https://www.fuzen.io/posts/vibe-coding-examples-2026)). A local-only demo "scores as if it doesn't work," so deploy to a URL ([lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)).

```
# Rules for every session
```

### Case-selection scorecard (apply once the five cases drop)

Score each case 1 to 5 on each row. Rows marked gate must score at least 3, or the case is out regardless of total.

| Criterion | Question | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Demo-ability (gate) | Can one screen show the AI doing something a person can't do in that time, on case data, by Saturday 6 pm? | x2 |
| Quantifiable impact (gate) | Can Parker put dollars, hours or error rate on it from case facts plus stated assumptions? | x2 |
| AI is necessary | Would rules or a spreadsheet do the same job? (5 = no) | x1 |
| Data available | Is data supplied, or easy to synthesize realistically, so the build isn't blocked? | x1 |
| Judge and sponsor fit | Does it touch supply chain, healthcare, finance, retail or operations, where the likely judges have KPIs? | x1 |
| Team edge | Does pre-med plus finance give a credible insider angle? | x1 |
| Crowding | Will most teams pick this case? (5 = probably not; low-confidence row) | x0.5 |

The demo-ability and impact gates come straight from the notes' synthesis, and sponsor-KPI fit reflects how sponsor-owned business cases are judged ([Alabama Culverhouse on the Dell case](https://culverhouse.ua.edu/?p=32025)). The crowding row gets half weight because no evidence shows that less crowded tracks win more often. Pick the top total, write the one-liner, and do not pivot after about 11:00 am. The 17% rule from 72-hour events maps to roughly the first two hours here ([lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)).

## Map every second of the video to a criterion and a required item

Judges spend about five minutes per Devpost submission and often watch the video first. Put key information up front, keep Devpost's default headings, and tailor each section to the criteria ([Biggin, dev.to](https://dev.to/jacklyn/how-to-make-your-devpost-submissions-not-suck-37i0)). Show working software, not slides. Production quality does not affect scores, and 2 to 3 minutes is the common target, with 4 as a ceiling ([AI Tinkerers video guide](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)). With no limit published, aim for **3:30** unless the form says otherwise.

| 2:35 to 3:10 | Parker: cost per task before and after, annual net savings, payback, conservative case | Impact | Impact |
| 3:10 to 3:30 | Limits named honestly, safeguards, pilot plan, next steps; recap the three key points | Limitations, next steps | Presentation, Impact |

The opening 30 seconds deserve the most rehearsal, because "that is where the score is set" ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). Close by repeating three or four memorable points, YC's "vertebrae" ([YC](https://ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)). If there is a live round, use the same order with the demo starting inside the first third of the slot. Keep the deck to about 8 to 10 slides with one point and roughly seven words each ([YC](https://ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/); [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)). Use action titles that state the takeaway, include a screenshot of the running product, and move the cost model, sensitivity table, architecture, eval results and privacy design to an appendix. In the Devpost write-up, add short "Business impact," "Responsible AI" and "Built this weekend vs pre-existing" paragraphs inside the default headings so every criterion has a findable answer. Make the first gallery image a product screenshot with the key result visible.

### Parker's finance and ROI slide framework

Executive judges want one workflow, one team and one measurable outcome, with bounded ranges rather than heroic claims ([Voltage Control](https://voltagecontrol.com/articles/how-to-get-executive-buy-in-for-ai-initiatives-without-overselling/)). Build one worked example Parker can defend line by line, because "judges see the presentation, not your spreadsheet" ([Roadtooffer](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/consulting-case-competition-guide)). Size any market bottom-up, as price times number of customers ([YC](https://ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)).

| Line | Formula | Source of inputs |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline cost | hours per task x tasks per year x loaded hourly cost, plus error or rework cost | Case facts; state assumptions |
| AI run cost | tokens per task x current API price, plus hosting, scaled to annual volume | Measure tokens in `eval/`; price from the provider page Saturday |
| Residual human cost | review minutes per task x volume x loaded cost | Your human-in-the-loop design |
| Annual net savings | baseline minus (AI run + human review + maintenance) | Computed |
| Payback | one-time implementation cost / monthly net savings | Pilot estimate |
| 3-year ROI | (3 x annual net savings minus total cost) / total cost | Computed |
| Sensitivity | adoption at 30 / 60 / 90 percent; accuracy at low / base / high | Show that the conservative case still pays back |
| Rollout | pilot with one team, success metric, go/no-go date, then scale | Implementation plan |

The slide title should be one sentence, such as "Cuts review time X% and pays back in N months." The big number goes in large type, with the conservative case visible beneath it. Cost per task, before and after, is the strongest single visual, because it connects the AI build to the dollar outcome in one glance. Do not invent industry benchmarks. Cite case figures or label numbers as assumptions, since no cross-use-case benchmark for AI cost per task exists in the notes.

### Likely judge questions and owned answers

One judge says he almost always asks three questions: who it is for, what was built this weekend, and what breaks first. Naming a weakness raised scores ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). Executives probe data handling and privacy ([Voltage Control](https://voltagecontrol.com/articles/how-to-get-executive-buy-in-for-ai-initiatives-without-overselling/)). Responsible-AI rubrics reward explicit disclosure of risks and misuse ([CGU](https://research.cgu.edu/hackathon/home/judging-rubric/)). Fill in the brackets with real numbers from the build.

| Question | Owner | Answer pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Who exactly is this for? | Parker | Name the persona, their current workflow and the pain number from the case |
| What did you build this weekend? | Aidan | "Scaffold and LLM wrapper were pre-built and disclosed; everything case-specific, the [pipeline, UI, eval] was built here," pointing to the commit history |
| Why does this need AI? | Aidan | The before and after: rules can't [read unstructured X / reason across Y]; show the side by side |
| How do you handle hallucinations? | Aidan | Grounded in [source]; every claim cites a row; tested on [N] cases at [X]% correct; low confidence routes to a human, who approves [high-stakes step] |
| Where does the data go? | Aidan | API tier with no training on inputs, PII stripped before the call, role-based access, audit log; production could run on private infrastructure like TCU's AI² hybrid setup and would go through the client's security review |
| What does it cost at scale? | Parker | $[X] per task, $[Y] per year at [Z] volume, against $[W] in staff time; caching and smaller models for simple steps cut it further |
| What's your biggest assumption risk? | Parker | Name it (usually adoption or accuracy), then point to the conservative case that still pays back in [N] months |
| What breaks first? | Aidan | Name it (unusual input formats, rate limits at peak) and the mitigation |
| Why not just use ChatGPT or Copilot? | Aidan | Off-the-shelf lacks [workflow integration, case data, guardrails, audit trail]; the model is swappable, so the edge is the workflow and data |
| Will people adopt it? | Parker | Fits the tool they already use, one-team pilot, tracked metric, human stays in the loop, so it supports the role rather than replacing it |
| Why not just cut costs another way? | Parker | Compare against the obvious alternative in one line and show why it doesn't reach the same metric |

Rehearse these twice, decide in advance who takes each question, and practice the handoffs. Teams on the SEC case circuit won Best Q&A awards for exactly this kind of complementary split ([Alabama Culverhouse](https://culverhouse.ua.edu/?p=32025)).

This competition's distinguishing feature is its audience, not its rubric. The Aidan-and-Parker split is not a compromise forced by the rugby game. It is the winning structure itself, because most of the 59 entrants will lean entirely toward business slides or entirely toward code.
