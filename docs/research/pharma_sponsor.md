## 3. Why exclusivity and pricing matter to a distributor (McKesson view)
- **Generic launch timing:** distributors earn their highest gross margins on generics. When a brand goes generic, the distributor's revenue drops sharply because the price falls 80 to 95%, but gross profit per unit can rise. The first 6 months (the 180-day exclusivity of the first generic filer) set the sourcing contracts. McKesson's ClarusONE sourcing venture, which buys generics, has to line up suppliers ahead of the launch.
- **Brand price changes:** distributor fee-for-service contracts are often a percentage of list price (WAC). Brand price increases, cuts and IRA maximum fair prices (first 10 drugs January 2026, next 15 January 2027) change both revenue and fee income. The IRA also creates cash-flow and refund mechanics for pharmacies.
- **Biosimilars and specialty:** biosimilars for Stelara, Humira, Eylea, Prolia and, later, Keytruda and Opdivo shift volume through specialty distribution and McKesson-owned practices (US Oncology Network, PRISM Vision). In those practices, under buy-and-bill rules, the drug chosen changes the practice's margin (ASP + 6%) and McKesson's.
- **Inventory and working capital:** McKesson must avoid holding high-priced brand stock at a list-price cut or a generic launch date.
- **Strategy stance for the CEO:** decide which products and makers to prioritize, where to sign generic sourcing and private label, how much to invest in biosimilar and specialty capacity, and how this compares with Cencora (more specialty and biosimilar exposure) and Cardinal.
