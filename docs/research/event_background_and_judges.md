# Neeley AI Forward: AI Innovation Competition (Oct 16-18, 2026): Event Background and Likely Judges

Research date: 2026-10-16. Devpost pages were fetched directly (WebFetch got HTTP 403; plain HTTP download with a browser user agent worked). No one was contacted.

## Q1. What is Neeley AI Forward, who runs it, what is its purpose, and has this competition run before?

### Takeaway
Neeley AI Forward is a school-wide AI initiative launched in January/February 2026, directed by Will Drover (Entrepreneurship and Innovation chair), with five pillars and an industry advisory board. This October 2026 AI Innovation Competition appears to be the first of its kind; I found no prior edition, past winners, or past cases.

### Cited Findings
**The initiative**
- Announced Feb 11, 2026 to "coordinate, support and advance" AI integration across Neeley teaching, research and operations, aligned with TCU's university-wide AI² enterprise. Dean Craig Crossland: Neeley aims "not just to adopt AI, but to help define how AI is used responsibly." ([Neeley news: launch](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/neeley-launches-ai-forward.php))
- Five pillars: Research (Drover), Faculty (Beata Jones), Student (Elijah Clark), Operations and Infrastructure (Eric Arseneau), External/Community (Zain Ali). ([launch](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/neeley-launches-ai-forward.php); [spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php))
- Drover led a cross-college AI task force in Fall 2025. ([launch](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/neeley-launches-ai-forward.php))
- Existing Neeley AI work it builds on: faculty-built custom GPTs, AI role-play simulations, student-led AI consulting projects with industry partners, AI fundamentals in all core courses. ([launch](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/neeley-launches-ai-forward.php))
- First-semester results (June 30, 2026 recap): 600+ stakeholders, 20+ events, Google AI micro-credentials across all Neeley majors, AI certificates in development, NAIL Club (~200 members), 12 AI Executive Education courses reaching 200+ executives, a ChatGPT Pro pilot plus on-prem open-source models, internal tools such as "Syllabus Hub" (400+ syllabi processed) and a registrar-endorsed scheduling app. ([spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php))
- Neeley is the business school partner on a federal research pursuit about AI for legacy energy infrastructure. ([spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php))
- Drover goal: "We want every Neeley student to graduate genuinely prepared for an AI-driven business world." He calls the initiative "an evolving portfolio of experiences compounded over time." The student club GAIN (Generative AI in Finance & Networking), founded by finance students Blake Steensland and Chase Merrell, was rebranded as NAIL and merged into AI Forward. ([TCU 360, Mar 24 2026](https://tcu360.com/2026/03/24/neeley-ai-forward-launches-to-prepare-business-students-post-graduation/))

**Core team** ([AI Forward team page](http://www.neeley.tcu.edu/research-faculty/ai-forward-initiative.php))
- Will Drover: Professor, Chair of Entrepreneurship and Innovation, Director of Neeley AI Forward, Dean's Advisor on AI and Digital Innovation. His research is in entrepreneurial finance (angel, VC, crowdfunding). He was a Field Editor of the Journal of Business Venturing and has 3,000+ citations. Recent AI work with Laura Huang on why AI adoption is uneven across tasks and roles was featured in MIT Sloan Management Review and the WSJ. A JBV paper uses AI to study perceived founder age in fundraising. ([TCU directory](https://www.tcu.edu/directory/profile/drover-will.php); [Neeley research highlights](https://neeley.tcu.edu/research-highlights))
- Eric Arseneau: Associate Professor of Professional Practice, Assistant Chair of E&I, Associate Director of AI Forward.
- Elijah Clark: Instructor of Marketing, Director of the Business Innovation Academy, Assistant Director of AI Forward.
- Beata Jones: Professor of Professional Practice, Business Information Systems.
- Zain Ali: Associate Professor of Professional Practice in IS and Supply Chain; Advisory Board Chair.

**This competition** (from the Devpost pages, fetched 2026-10-16: [overview](https://neeley-ai-forward.devpost.com/), [rules](https://neeley-ai-forward.devpost.com/rules), [resources](https://neeley-ai-forward.devpost.com/resources), [schedule](https://neeley-ai-forward.devpost.com/details/dates))
- Title: "Neeley AI Forward: AI Innovation Competition." It brings "students, alumni, and industry professionals together to explore how AI can solve real business challenges." "No coding experience is required!"
- Teams of 2-5 with at least one Neeley student; TCU students only; invite only; 59 registered participants as of the fetch.
- Two tracks: **Emerging** and **Advanced**. Each team picks one track and one case.
- Prizes: $10,000 total, 5 winners (3 Emerging, 2 Advanced). Devpost labels it "1 non-cash prize" while the text says "cash prizes."
- Schedule: virtual kickoff Friday Oct 16; in person Sat-Sun at Neeley. Workshops run all day Saturday Oct 17, with "industry professionals" available as mentors. Submissions are due **Sunday Oct 18, 2:00 pm CDT**. Winners are announced **Oct 18, 5:00 pm CDT**.
- Required video: introduce team, track and case; explain problem and solution; demo the project and how it uses AI; highlight impact plus limitations and next steps.
- Rules: the solution must "meaningfully use AI." Projects must be built during the event, and existing resources must be disclosed. Mentors can guide, but teams do their own work. Judges' decisions are final.
- **Judging criteria (six, no weights published):** Innovation and Creativity; Technical Complexity; Completeness (works, no major bugs); Design (user-friendly, visually appealing); Impact (real problem, real-world use); Presentation (clearly explain problem, solution, and technical choices).
- **Judges:** listed only as "AI Industry Leaders," with no names.
- **Sponsors (Devpost logos):** Neeley AI Forward; Neeley Center for Supply Chain Innovation; Neeley Institute for Entrepreneurship and Innovation; **Dallas AI**.
- TCU calendar: Spencer Hays Hall, Business Commons 2303 AB. Organizing groups are Entrepreneurship and Innovation and Information Systems and Supply Chain Management. Co-listed departments include the Center for Supply Chain Innovation, IEI, AI Forward, and the undergraduate and graduate programs. The calendar URL slug is "neeley-ai-for-everyone-hack-a-thon." ([TCU calendar event](https://calendar.tcu.edu/event/neeley-ai-for-everyone-hack-a-thon); [CSCI calendar](https://calendar.tcu.edu/department/center_for_supply_chain_innovation))

**Related prior events**
- Inaugural Neeley AI Symposium, April 29, 2026, ~150 attendees. Panel moderated by Zain Ali with advisory board members; 20 industry partners ran mentoring sessions. ([Neeley AI Symposium story](https://internal.neeley.tcu.edu/News/Events/AI-Symposium); [spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php))
- Business Innovation Academy (July 2026): 80 high-school juniors and seniors built and pitched AI-driven business ideas. ([spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php))
- Chancellor's Innovation Prize (inaugural, spring 2026, campuswide, operational AI). The student category was won by Matt Neves (finance plus E&I major) with an AI platform that predicts and incentivizes attendance at TCU Athletics events. ([spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php); [TCU news](https://www.tcu.edu/news/2026/tcu-launches-chancellors-innovation-prize-focused-on-operational-ai.php))

### Inferences
- This is the first AI Forward hackathon. The format (tracks, Devpost, $10K) is new, so no "past winner" template exists. The closest proxy is the Chancellor's Prize winner: a finance student's practical, data-driven, measurable-outcome tool for a real TCU operation.
- The "no coding required" framing plus a Technical Complexity criterion suggests the Advanced track will reward real builds. A team with a working AI app (the pre-med builder) likely fits Advanced, where only 2 of 5 prizes sit but the field is probably thinner.
- Supply Chain Center co-sponsorship plus the organizing department (IS and Supply Chain) suggests at least one case is likely supply chain or operations themed.
- The required "limitations and next steps" item and Crossland's "responsibly" language suggest judges will look for honest risk and responsible-AI discussion.

### Gaps
- The five cases (topics, companies) are not public. The Devpost overview mentions tracks but no case list, and the registration page is empty publicly. Cases likely release at Friday's virtual kickoff.
- Criterion weights, how tracks differ in expectations, and whether pitches are live or video-only for finalists are all unpublished.
- No prior editions found.

## Q3. AI themes Neeley/TCU emphasizes, and likely case industries

### Takeaway
TCU's AI story centers on AI² (a $10M Dell-anchored initiative with AWS and Microsoft), operational AI, AI literacy for every student, and responsible use. Case industries are unpublished, but the sponsors and board point to supply chain/operations, healthcare distribution, retail, tech, financial services, and real estate.

### Cited Findings

Partners are Dell Technologies (Dell AI Factory with NVIDIA on campus), AWS, and Microsoft. It uses a hybrid on-prem/cloud design so sensitive data can stay local, and it is tied to TCU's R1 push. ([Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/tcu-launches-10m-ai%c2%b2-initiative-with-dell-to-scale-campuswide-use/); [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/tcu-partners-with-dell-to-launch-major-campuswide-ai-initiative); [TCU news](https://tcu.edu/news/2025/new-ai-initiative-to-be-an-immediate-boost-to-tcu.php))
- Chancellor's Innovation Prize focuses on "operational AI": advising, workflows, data access and decision support, resource allocation and scheduling, staff productivity. Chancellor Daniel Pullin and Tom Wavering (chief strategy and innovation officer) are named. ([TCU news](https://www.tcu.edu/news/2026/tcu-launches-chancellors-innovation-prize-focused-on-operational-ai.php))
- Neeley tools in use: Google AI micro-credentials, ChatGPT Pro pilot, NotebookLM, Breakout Learning. ([spring recap](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/ai-forward-spring-recap.php))
- Symposium themes: agentic AI, practical applications, cybersecurity and workforce strategy, and balancing optimism with realism about current capabilities. ([Symposium](https://internal.neeley.tcu.edu/News/Events/AI-Symposium))
- Center for Supply Chain Innovation framed its 2024 Global Supply Chain Summit around integrating AI into supply chain practice (older info). ([Neeley news 2024](https://neeley.tcu.edu/news/posts/2024-global-supply-chain-summit.php))

This is more likely than the generic Fort Worth list (American Airlines, Lockheed, BNSF, Bell). Energy is plausible given the legacy energy infrastructure research pursuit.

## Q4. Other Neeley competitions and their judging norms

### Takeaway
Neeley's flagship, Values and Ventures, rewards for-profit ventures that solve a social or environmental problem while returning profit. Its judges are entrepreneurs, investors and CEOs. The Chancellor's Prize rewarded a practical operational AI tool from a finance student.

### Cited Findings
- Richards Barrentine Values and Ventures (run by IEI, which also sponsors this event): 2026 finals were April 9-11. Prizes: $40K grand, $25K second, $15K third, plus five $2,500 honorable mentions. Ventures must be for-profit and values-centered ("conscious capitalism"). Judges and mentors include seasoned entrepreneurs, investment professionals and CEOs (750+ since 2011). Special awards include one for superior returns with environmental responsibility. ([V&V site](https://www.neeley.tcu.edu/VandV); [V&V program page](https://neeley.tcu.edu/centers-institutes/institute-for-entrepreneurship-innovation/values-ventures/index.php))

### Inferences
- IEI-style judging rewards a clear customer, business model, financial viability, and values or impact. The finance teammate should own market sizing, unit economics, and ROI. The builder should own the live demo and the technical choices, since Technical Complexity and Completeness are explicit criteria here.

### Gaps
- No published V&V scoring rubric. 2026 V&V winners and judges were not confirmed.
