## Sharpest Needle candidate

**#1, the Enbrel true-up (AMGN-nugget-002).** It names one product, one number (+16 points of deduction true-up, about $97M) and one quarter, and nobody raised it: Enbrel was not mentioned by any speaker on the Q2 call. It also connects the case product, the IRA force and the quality of Amgen's Q2 beat in a single sentence. It can be proven wrong soon: if the Q3 2026 10-Q shows Enbrel down about 20% or worse, the Q2 "-4%" was accounting, not stabilization. It beats the runner-up, the IRS dispute (#4), on obscurity and certainty. #4 carries larger dollars, but the CFO read it in Q1 prepared remarks, and its outcome turns on a court ruling we cannot estimate. #2 should go next to #1 in the deck, because it shows the team caught the code's receivables flag and found its cause instead of reporting it as channel stuffing.

## Part 2 hooks

- Enbrel cash is lower than the Q2 print suggests (#1, #3). Do not size the obesity build-out on a stabilizing Enbrel. The Q3 2026 Enbrel YoY is the kill signal.
- Hold a $3B to $7B tax-payment reserve before any debt-funded deal (#4). The IRS has now opened 2019-2022.
- The MFN launch-pricing clause and the tariff-relief lapse in about December 2028 (#11) cap the US price premium on any new launch, MariTide included as background.
- Prefer wholly owned assets or buy back partner economics over new 50/50 deals (#9).
- Do not count on US pharmacy-benefit biosimilars as the Enbrel and Prolia hedge (#5). PBM private labels have shut WEZLANA and AMJEVITA out.
- Diligence lesson from TAVNEOS (#7): acquired-data risk turned a 2022 deal into an FDA misstatement allegation.
- Repatha bridge (#6, #10): require a disclosed quarterly price and volume split. A 2027 price guide below mid-single digits is the warning.
