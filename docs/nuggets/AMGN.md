## Sharpest Needle candidate

**#1, the IRS Puerto Rico case (AMGN-nugget-006).** Headline: $7.6B claimed plus undisclosed interest vs $6.3B reserved for all open years (UTB $4.4B plus $1.9B accrued interest and penalties): at least $1.3B unreserved. The base is that $1.3B floor (EPS -2.39), before any claim interest and before the 2016-2018 NOPA. The $3.2B gap (7.6 less the $4.4B UTB alone, EPS -5.88) is shown only as the bear case, and $7.0B with 2016-2018 added is the severe bear. The decision is expected late 2026 or early 2027, inside the 24-month window. No analyst has asked about it on any of the three calls, the Q2 call skipped it, and the sharper fact is stated nowhere: the UTB did not grow for the 2016-2018 NOPA (prior-year additions of $14M in 2025; H1 2026 additions are all current-year). Priced-in defense: the dispute has been public since 2017, and a GAAP reserve below the IRS ask is normal (more-likely-than-not measurement), so the claim is not that the reserve is wrong; it is the floor, the near decision date and the flat reserve. It is a one-off binary tax item, which is why it scores 3 of 5 and why the slide must show the bull at 0. It beats the round 1 needle, the Enbrel true-up (#4), because the true-up is about $97M, reverses inside the same half, and is a timing swing. Bull case is real: Amgen says the IRS's own Tax Court positions were "more favorable to Amgen" than its audit team's, so a partial loss is plausible.

## Challenger round 2

Brief: find the best AMGN needle, resolve the red team's Q2 vs H1 inconsistency, test the IRS case and the deductions trend, hunt five more leads, and apply the red team's fixes.

**1. The Q2 vs H1 Enbrel inconsistency is a bridge reclassification, not a restatement and not a nugget error.** Revenue reconciles exactly: Q1 320 + Q2 580 = H1 900, and Q1 2025 510 + Q2 2025 604 = 1,114 (10-Q Q1 p. 36, 10-Q Q2 p. 35). The percentage bridges do not add: deductions -92 + 97 = +5 vs H1 6% x 1,114 = +67; price -77 + -133 = -210 vs H1 25% x 1,114 = -279. About $62M to $69M moves from "deductions" to "net selling price" in the six-month view. The same pattern appears at four other brands: Repatha's Q1 +8% deduction item and TEPEZZA's Q2 +4% item vanish from their six-month bridges, and Otezla's opposite-sign quarterly items net out. So Amgen's six-month bridge nets estimate changes that reverse within the year and books the rest as price. Effect on the needle: the Q2 true-up is real but is mostly Q1's charge coming back, and part of Q1's "one-time" charge was recurring price. AMGN-002 is demoted to row #4.
