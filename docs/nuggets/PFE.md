## Sharpest Needle candidate

**PFE-nugget-001, the Eliquis US price windfall.** Pfizer's largest product grew 25% in the US in Q2 2026 because a 2026 list-price cut lowered rebates. Only BMS's 10-Q says so. Pfizer calls it "pricing dynamics", and when Chris Schott asked how much of the raised guidance came from Eliquis, the interim CFO said "not one single driver". The lift is about $0.9B of near-pure margin a year ($0.14 EPS, about 5% of guidance), and it stops when settled generics launch on April 1, 2028, inside the case's 24-month window.

It passes the round-1 checks. Against two prior years it is a real outlier (2024 +14% on demand, 2025 +7% with lower net price). It pays off inside the window. "List price" gets 0 hits in four Pfizer calls and every Pfizer filing. The peer column is BMS's own 10-Q. The scope is US-only on both sides. The base case rests on two labelled assumptions, not three.

Priced-in defense: BMS's list-price cut is public, and analysts know BMS guides Eliquis to 20% to 25% growth. What the market misses is the size and the expiry inside Pfizer's own P&L: a price-led gain of about $0.9B a year, set against a $1.5B non-COVID guidance raise that management would not split, disappears in April 2028, so the 2026 to 2027 base that the LOE erodes is inflated.

It beats the runner-up, the flat US Vyndaqel line against Amvuttra and Attruby (002), because 002's dollar impact needs a counterfactual growth rate and a margin assumption, and competition from Attruby is a known theme. 001 is an observed dollar change with a contractual end date.
