## Sharpest Needle candidate

**Row 1, the DARZALEX royalty.** It is J&J's largest product (15.0% of revenue), the number is new in the FY2025 10-K and absent from FY2024, it sits in a footnote-style Item 1 paragraph that no analyst has asked about, and it carries a dated mechanism: roughly $1.4B a year of cost of goods (about $0.47 a share) that the CEO says disappears in 2029. It also ties the label to the numbers, because the same paragraph says the Genmab patents expire in 2029 while J&J owns separate DARZALEX FASPRO patents, which is why the royalty can end while the franchise keeps going. It beats the runner-up, the rebate true-up disclosure (row 2), because row 2 has a stronger peer contrast (3.0% vs Lilly's under 1%) but an unknown sign: we cannot yet say whether it flatters or depresses revenue, so a judge could fairly call it a question rather than a finding. The honest pitch pairs row 1 with row 8: the 2029 royalty tailwind may be offset by an IRA Part B price on the same drug in the same year, which is the kind of second-order read the case asks for.

## Part 2 hooks

- Use the 2029 DARZALEX royalty roll-off (row 1) to fund Stelara-replacement launches, but only after CMS's early-2027 selection list clears DARZALEX (row 8).
- Gate further immunology spend on the first separately reported ICOTYDE quarter, promised for 2027 (row 5).
- Size any acquisition to the combined STELARA plus second-wave US gap, about $4.2B annualized ((654 + 390) x 4), not STELARA alone (row 3).
- Treat ERLEADA as a 2028 cash product, not a growth pillar (row 6).
- Judge capital returns on EPS after recurring litigation, not adjusted EPS (row 4). Kill signal: the ovarian talc deal missing its 95% threshold.
- Kill signal for the US growth story: rebate true-ups falling back under 2% in the next 10-Q (row 2).
- Assume launch-price parity with other developed countries for every new US launch under the confidential government deal (row 9).
