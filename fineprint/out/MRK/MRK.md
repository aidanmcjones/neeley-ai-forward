## Sharpest Needle candidate

**Kept: MRK-nugget-001, the net 2027 one-year margin peak.** Headline: Merck's 2027 margin carries a one-year royalty gift of $0.5B to $0.8B net ($0.54B to $0.79B, $0.19 to $0.27 EPS), which the Dec 2028 Keytruda cliff takes back. The 2.5% royalty to BMS and Ono on all Keytruda and Keytruda Qlex sales ends Dec 31, 2026; the gross lift is $821M ($0.28), and the net figure subtracts Winrevair's 22% royalty on 2027 growth and a possible European Qlex royalty and adds the vaccine royalty step-down. It is the only MRK finding that is contractual, footnote-only (zero "royalt" hits on Merck's four calls and zero Keytruda mentions on BMS's four) and confirmed from the counterparty's own filing.

**Priced-in defense, one sentence:** the royalty schedule has been public since 2017, so the claim is not that the royalty ends; the claim is that 2027 is a one-time margin peak, in the last full year before the Dec 2028 cliff, and every 2028 model that starts from 2027 margin overstates the base.

**BMS proof box:** BMS's H1 2026 Keytruda royalty income is $308M, so $308M x 2 / 0.75 = $821M gross on Merck's side, which implies $32.8B of 2026 Keytruda sales under the 2.5% rate. The 2025 check is within 1%: BMS $588M / 0.75 = $784M against Merck's 2.5% x $31,680M = $792M. One-line answer to the BMS twin (BMY-001): for BMS it is an other-income loss below operating income, smaller than the Eliquis Europe step it already guides; for Merck it is a cost-of-sales gift that inflates the base year of every 2028 cliff model.

**Runner-up Lynparza (012), restated:** earliest generic approval is Sept 2027 (compound patent expiry); actual entry depends on 2029 product patents in litigation, awaiting a trial date, and possibly March 2028 if six months of pediatric exclusivity applies. It is a floor date with three stacked conditions, so it stays a Low-confidence row and does not go on the needle slide.
